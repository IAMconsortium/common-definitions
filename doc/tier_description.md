# Variable tiers
## Background

The variable definitions of the IAMC data template, hosted on the common-definitions repostiory, include a tier system that allows constructing a new draft data template for a model intercomparison project (MIP) in a simple and efficient manner. For each topical area of the variable definitions, maintained by a subgroup of the IAMC Scientific Working Group (SWG) on data protocols and management, several tiers are defined that in the highest tier (tier 1) include variables that are generally expected to be reported under almost all Integrated Assessment Model (IAM) MIPs that cover that topic while the lower tiers (2, 3, etc.) include variables with increasing specificity that would usually only be reported in MIPs with a focus on the respective topical area. 

## Use cases
A typical use of the tier system for constructing a new template is illustrated by the following example, based on four out of 10 topical areas socio-econmic, energy, transport and emissions with three tier levels 1-3 where 1 covers high-level variables and 3 very detailed reporting. Let's assume a study that focuses on air quality implications of the transport sector. From the four topical areas mentioned above, very detailed information for the transport sector, its energy use and corresponding emissions across different species would be needed, translating into tier 3. Socio-economic reporting would only be needed at relatively aggregate level, i.e. tier 1. The draft template therefore might thus be composed of tiers 1-3 for the energy, emissions and transport variables and tier 1 socio-econmic variables. As every MIP typically has its own reporting needs, defined by its research questions, variables from the draft template may be removed while others from lower tiers may be added manually. Finally, additional variables that are not covered in the common-definitions repostiory can be defined in a project-specifc repository and if relevant for other applications may be proposed to the relevant SWG subgroup for addition to the common-definitions repository. This procedure is aiming at an faster and more coherent template construction process compared to a fully manual compilation of the data template based on the full list of varaibles in common-definitions.

Note that each MIP itself may assign tiers to variables that communicate to the participating modeling teams the priority of submitting specific variables. These MIP internal tiers do not need to align with the above described tier for the template in common-definitions that assisst with the template construction process.

## Tier definitions
The allocation of the different tier levels are proposed by the the subgroups and discussed and reviewed by the team of subgroup chairs to ensure coherence of the approach across the topical areas. The following initial high-level guidance serves as a basis for the assignment of tiers and will be monitored and revised as necessary with growing experience of the use of the tier systems.

* tier 1: high-level variables that are relevant for system charactereization and are generally expected to be reported by any MIP with full system coverage, only up to one level of breakdown for variables (e.g, emissions by species, final energy by fuel, land cover by type, GDP as MER and PPP), number of variables ~10-100, in some areas such as energy that covers many aspects (e.g., primary, secondary, final energy) the set of tier 1 variables might include significantly more variables
* tier 2: More detailed breakdown of variables, generally into two levels, (e.g. emissions by species and sector; final energy consumption by sector and fuel) number of variables ~100-1000
* tier 3: Very detailed breakdown of variables, generally into three or more levels (e.g. population by gender, age cohort and education; final energy by sector, fuel and application), number of variables >~1000
